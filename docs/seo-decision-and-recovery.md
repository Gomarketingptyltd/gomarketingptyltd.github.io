# SEO Decision and Recovery Protocol

Updated 2026-10-08. This protocol governs review decisions; it is not a deployed monitoring service, a new scheduler or permission to change accounts, spend money or publish unapproved material.

## Objective and boundaries

Optimize qualified enquiries and viable quotation opportunities from supported markets. Organic rankings, AI citations, impressions and clicks are intermediate signals. Preserve the current form, confidentiality restrictions and production safety requirements in `docs/seo-manager-operating-system.md`.

Keep company performance figures, hypothesis records, contact information and approval details in ignored `.search-console/reports/` files. Public records may describe methods and verified non-sensitive releases only.

## Evidence gates

1. Validate the measurement. Use the same country, search type, aggregation and comparable final-data periods. Check authentication, reporting gaps, tracking consent, sample size and query anonymisation. Missing data is unknown, not zero. Query/page rows and property totals are not interchangeable.
2. Validate the release. Confirm the intended change is live, then check the relevant engine's indexed/crawled version. Do not attribute old-version rankings to an unrecrawled edit. A recent crawl is necessary evidence, not proof every new element influenced ranking.
3. Isolate the bottleneck. Separate technical access, demand, search intent, visibility, clicks, enquiries and sales follow-up. Multiple pages appearing for a query do not by themselves prove harmful competition.
4. State a hypothesis with disconfirming evidence. A plausible explanation is not a diagnosis. Record at least one alternative cause and the next check that distinguishes them.
5. Preselect the intervention and fallback. Specify scope, outcome, observation window, stop condition, owner and any approval needed before changing content.
6. Verify and decide. Mark continue, revise, switch, rollback or close with evidence. A completed task is not evidence of commercial success.

For this low-volume site, prefer sequential, documented tests over split tests that divide already scarce traffic. Change one major acquisition hypothesis per page at a time. A related bilingual change is one release. Keep unrelated pages stable as contextual comparisons, not randomized controls or proof of causation. Record seasonality, search updates, promotions and other concurrent changes as confounders.

## Trigger and fallback matrix

The durations and thresholds below are operational triage rules, not search-engine requirements or statistical significance claims. Dates are evaluated during available authorized runs, not a promise of continuous monitoring.

| Condition | Trigger and evidence | First response | Fallback and exit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Production regression | Confirmed broken layout, critical URL/access error, accidental noindex/canonical change or broken contact path | Stop new publishing; reproduce on affected URLs; compare release diff with last verified version | Restore only the defective owned change or known-good affected artifact, preserve unrelated work, re-test desktop/mobile and live URLs. No destructive whole-worktree reset |
| Data/account failure | Missing dates, revoked access, mismatched filters or conflicting attribution | Repair measurement/access within existing permission; record unknown values; reuse only suitable complete data | Continue independent public-site checks and approved content preparation. Do not infer rankings from stale data or change DNS/permissions as a workaround |
| Material search decline | For a comparable complete 28-day site/intent segment with at least 200 prior impressions: decline of at least 30% and at least 50 impressions is a review trigger. Smaller commercially important segments still receive manual review | During the next available run, split by page/query/device and check crawl/indexing, release dates, demand and search-system events | If page-specific, investigate intent/technical changes. If site-wide, review broader quality/access and external events. Do not automatically undo the latest release or diagnose an algorithm penalty |
| Updated page not recrawled | Check at the next weekly review; if still stale 14 days after a material release, escalate discovery diagnosis | Inspect canonical, crawl access, sitemap inclusion and real contextual internal links; check request ledger | One eligible request only if not already accepted. Work on approved distribution or another page while waiting; do not repeatedly change dates/titles |
| Indexed but little relevant exposure | Review at 28 days after observed recrawl, or earlier when intent mismatch is visible. Very small samples remain inconclusive | Check whether real buyers use the targeted phrasing; inspect current result types and related queries; compare demand cautiously | Focus on a narrower genuine buyer problem using the existing owner page where possible. Reallocate production effort instead of creating near-duplicate city/keyword pages |
| Exposure but weak clicks | Comparable query/position/device segments, actual displayed result and sufficient observations; not a universal CTR threshold | Check title/snippet accuracy, relevance and result features; improve one diagnosed message issue | If SERP intent is different, change audience/content strategy rather than endless headline rotations. Wait for recrawl before evaluating another snippet treatment |
| Visits but few qualified enquiries | Validated human-relevant traffic, contact-path availability and private lead counts; a few visits alone are inconclusive | Review promise, service fit, CTA clarity and follow-up using approved information; no form mutation or test submission | Test a more specific service explanation and existing contact route. Investigate measurement before blaming the page. Keep customer identifiers out of analytics |
| Enquiries but wrong fit | Repeated privately verified patterns in service, geography or expectations | Clarify who the service suits, relevant exclusions and what is scoped; use aggregate reasons only | Tighten targeting and distribution. Do not increase broad traffic as a substitute for qualification |
| Qualified enquiries but weak quotation/sales progression | Sufficient owner-supplied aggregate outcomes and timings; attribution is not assumed | Review response handling, scope clarity and next steps with the owner | Improve private sales/process materials with permission. Do not report an SEO failure or publish prices/customer cases to solve a sales-stage issue |
| AI access but no observed citations | Platform-specific eligibility checked; recurring fixed non-brand prompts and available official reports, with unknowns marked | Identify which sources answer the buyer question, factual gaps and genuine original value | Improve existing explanations and legitimate third-party discovery; test relevant questions. Do not build mass AI-only pages, buy mentions or treat one prompt as market share |
| AI citation with incorrect facts | Stored answer, cited URL and verified company source disagree | Find whether the error originates in company copy, an outdated external source or the model's inference | Correct owned facts or prepare a request to the responsible publisher; recheck retrieval. Never claim direct control of a model's answers |

Before suspecting a systemic ranking event, check Google's status history and data anomalies. Timing correlation alone does not establish the cause. Compare demand using longer historical data and appropriate tools where available; a low-volume Trends result does not prove absent demand. See [Google's traffic-drop diagnosis guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/debugging-search-traffic-drops) and [Search performance definitions](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en).

## Three fallback levels

### Recover

Fix a verified safety, access, measurement or discovery defect. This takes priority over new ranking experiments. Restore the smallest necessary change, document what was affected and verify live outcomes. Do not roll back solely because an average position moved.

### Refocus

When a retrieved page does not satisfy the observed buyer intent, change the offer explanation or the target problem. Existing service owners remain the default. A new page needs a distinct buyer task and unique useful content; merging/removing URLs needs evidence, a redirect plan and explicit review.

Prepare fallback briefs while the primary experiment runs. For each brief, record the destination, approved factual boundaries, asset status and success criterion. A brief is not a finished asset or permission to publish.

### Diversify

Run approved owned-channel distribution and relevant relationship development alongside SEO from the start, rather than waiting for organic failure. Keep platform and channel results separate. A paid-search pilot is optional and requires an owner-approved total cap, geography, conversion definition and stop rule. Never switch silently to paid traffic or label it organic growth.

Do not use confidential client material, incentive reviews, fake third-party recommendations, mass outreach, purchased ranking links or hidden AI-targeted instructions as a fallback.

## Portfolio and review limits

- Keep at most two active material website growth experiments. Read-only diagnosis, safety fixes and already-approved distribution can run alongside them. Safety work always takes precedence.
- Reserve work for both acquiring attention and converting it. During a recrawl hold, prepare the next approved promotion, improve the evidence base or diagnose another independent bottleneck; do not repeatedly restate the hold.
- Every hold has a specific date and evidence trigger. Two consecutive reviews with no new evidence require a different diagnostic step or a ready independent acquisition task. Access/approval blockers remain explicit; they do not authorize bypassing boundaries.
- At four weeks, review delivery, access, recrawl and leading signals. If the intended intervention never reached the audience, classify the constraint rather than declaring content failure.
- At eight weeks, review commercial outcomes and relative channel/service opportunities. Reallocate discretionary work from unproductive hypotheses even when a statistical claim is impossible; label this a business decision, not causal proof.
- At twelve weeks, explicitly continue, redesign or stop discretionary expansion for each workstream. Keep essential service pages and safety maintenance. Do not restart the entire timeline by changing another headline.
- The owner sets acceptable acquisition cost and commercial targets once aggregate baseline, margin and capacity are available. Never invent enquiry volume or financial benchmarks.

## Run output and responsibility

Each weekly review must update the private register with:

- Current bottleneck, evidence, confidence and competing explanations.
- What was actually shipped, published, measured or diagnosed; exact evidence location.
- Page mutation status and separate workstream state.
- Primary outcome, guardrail results and whether the sample supports a conclusion.
- Continue/revise/switch/rollback/close decision, selected fallback and next review date.
- Approval or access requests consolidated for the owner; no requests for information already available safely.

The agent owns research, technical diagnosis, scoped drafts, tests, verification and truthful reporting. The owner retains control of credentials/verification, spending, account permissions, approved outbound publication and any exceptional confidentiality decision. No form delivery tests or changes resume without permission.

The existing scheduler cadence is not modified by these documents. Automated live alerts, diagnosis rules in dashboard code and additional account integrations are separate implementation work; do not describe this written protocol as those systems being deployed.
