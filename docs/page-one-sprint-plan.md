## Sprint goals

### Primary goal for 2026-06-29

- Homepage reaches position 8-12 or shows sustained CTR improvement while holding position 10-15.
- `services/digital.html` reaches position 8-12 or shows sustained CTR improvement while holding position 10-15.
- `services/chineseCommunityGrowth.html` holds or improves its current page-2 edge and moves closer to position 10.

### Secondary goal for 2026-06-29

- `services/support.html` and `services/advertising.html` gain impressions without slipping materially in relevance.
- `services/sydneyBilingualMarketingAgency.html` and `services/marketingAutomationServicesSydney.html` receive continued indexing and internal-link support.

### Longer-range goal

- The first realistic page-1 breakthrough should come from the homepage, `services/digital.html`, or `services/chineseCommunityGrowth.html`.
- Support and advertising are expected to need a longer runway than the top three candidates.

## Execution rhythm

### Review cadence

- Monday at 09:15 AEST: full weekly review
- Thursday at 09:15 AEST: pulse check

### Edit cadence

- Make material copy changes once every 10-14 days, not after every review.
- Change only 1-2 pages per edit cycle unless a major indexing or technical issue appears.
- After any material page rewrite, submit the priority URLs through Search Console URL Inspection on the same day.

## Decision rules

Use these rules to decide whether to edit, hold, or only submit indexing requests:

### Rule 1: positions 8-15 with weak CTR

- Tighten title tag, meta description, first-screen copy, and first two FAQ items.
- Do not rewrite the whole page.

### Rule 2: positions 15-25 with rising impressions

- Refine intro copy, internal links, heading language, and query alignment.
- Keep the page focused on the existing target theme rather than broadening scope.

### Rule 3: positions worse than 25 without a strong rise in impressions

- Do not keep rewriting the page.
- Prioritise internal links, Search Console request indexing, and patience for more data.

### Rule 4: no meaningful change in fresh data

- Hold copy changes.
- Keep collecting data until the next Monday review.

## Weekly operating sequence

### Week 1: 2026-06-01 to 2026-06-07

- Publish and index the updated `support`, `advertising`, and `digital` pages.
- Review fresh Search Console and sitemap status.
- Keep homepage, digital, and Chinese Community Growth as the active sprint pages.

### Week 2: 2026-06-08 to 2026-06-14

- Check which of the top three pages gained impressions or improved position.
- Refine only the pages already moving.
- Do not open new optimisation fronts unless a new query signal is clearly stronger.

### Week 3: 2026-06-15 to 2026-06-21

- Strengthen click intent on any active target page sitting around positions 8-20.
- Tighten FAQ wording and first-screen copy around the real query, not assumed keywords.
- Re-check indexing support pages and confirm internal-link paths still make sense.

### Week 4: 2026-06-22 to 2026-06-29

- Compare performance against the sprint baseline.
- Keep pushing only the pages still gaining traction.
- Freeze low-signal rewrite ideas and carry forward only the highest-probability pages.

## What not to do during this sprint

- Do not publish lots of glossary or definition articles.
- Do not redesign the homepage again.
- Do not change many pages at once without fresh Search Console data.
- Do not chase low-intent topics that are not tied to service demand.
- Do not treat a tiny movement in average position as proof that the whole site improved or declined.

## Success signals

Early positive signals:

- impressions continue rising on homepage, digital, and Chinese Community Growth
- homepage or digital move into the 8-12 range
- CTR improves on pages already sitting close to page 1
- Search Console continues to show clean sitemap submission and usable auth status

Stronger signals:

- at least one primary ranking target reaches page 1 or sits consistently on the edge of page 1
- clicks continue rising on homepage and digital
- support and advertising pick up better query alignment without needing large rewrites
